# Biblia: a research map for conversation review

[中文](../biblia.md) · **English** · [日本語](../ja/biblia.md) · [Home](../../README.en.md)

Conversation review draws on Conversation Analysis (CA). The repository supplies cue extraction, judgment storage, and interfaces for later use. Users configure the analysis procedure; papers and private analysis materials are not included.

This guide is a sanitized, citation-free adaptation of the original deployment's literature map. It identifies the kinds of research that may help answer each question. It lists no authors, years, titles, quotations, or literature links, and contains no private annotations, real conversation examples, or reading history. Users choose literature and add their own local paths and reading locations.

The map includes CA and neighboring work on pragmatics, politeness, and cross-language interaction. Check the subject matter and methods of each study; these fields do not form a single set of decision rules.

## Research directions by question

| Review question | Relevant kind of literature | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Is this a question, confirmation, disagreement, or move toward closing? | How utterances are understood as actions in particular positions | What the prior turn makes relevant and how the next turn responds; sentence form versus action |
| The sentence makes sense alone but does not connect in conversation | Sequence organization, adjacent responses, insertions, expansions | Whether the unit of analysis must include neighboring turns; which unfinished action the response addresses |
| What is being corrected, and who first notices the problem? | Self-repair, other-initiated repair, repair across languages | Trouble location, initiation, who completes repair, and whether subsequent interaction succeeds |
| Understanding seemed adequate, but trouble appeared one turn later | Understanding problems and repair revealed beyond the next turn | How a later response reinterprets earlier understanding; preserve the prior judgment and the basis of change |
| Can spoken-conversation methods be applied to text chat? | Comparisons of instant messaging and spoken repair | Editing, sending, message splitting, reply placement; visible sent records versus invisible composition |
| What do short responses like “嗯”, “对”, or “そう” convey? | Response tokens, recipient conduct, displays of understanding, information receipt | Position and subsequent action; receipt alone may not indicate agreement |
| Does continuing without objection count as agreement? | Agreement/disagreement in assessments, response forms, sequential position | An expressed stance versus simply continuing; silence or continuation cannot supply missing confirmation |
| The story received a response, but did the response acknowledge its emotion or stance? | Empathy, stance in storytelling, activity alignment, affective affiliation | Helping the story proceed versus endorsing the teller's assessment; evidence for each |
| Why does a response to a story feel inappropriate? | Storytelling sequences, recipient participation, turn design | How stories begin, develop, and end; whether to ask, assess, continue, or yield the turn |
| When someone describes a problem, should the response ask more or move toward another topic? | Troubles-telling and gradual topic transitions | How transitions occur through adjacent responses; the analyst's preference does not establish completion |
| Who knows this, and why was a guess stated as fact? | Epistemic status, rights to knowledge, and stance in interaction | Firsthand knowledge, reports, inference, and unknowns; confidence versus evidential basis |
| The explanation is thorough, but feels confusing or patronizing | Native/non-native interaction, explanation strategies and reception | What understanding the recipient actually displays; matching detail and expression to their needs |
| Could this threaten face, or is politeness being mistaken for agreement? | Facework, politeness, interactional relationships | Effects of relationship and setting; polite form does not establish approval or inner emotion |
| Is bodily, spatial, or shared-environment information missing from the text? | Multimodal interaction, embodied action, everyday practices | What observable materials the study used; do not invent gaze, movement, or intonation from text alone |

## Connecting it to review

1. Read the transcript and neighboring turns first. State the particular uncertainty, then choose a research direction.
2. Configure your own literature. Check its participants, language, medium, and data conditions. A research topic listed here does not by itself justify a conclusion.
3. Bring the study's distinctions back to the original sequence. Record support from subsequent responses when available; retain “unknown” otherwise.
4. Store initial judgment and rereading separately, with evidence, reading location, what changed, and remaining uncertainty. Tools can then consume the resulting judgments in scoring or reflection.

Do not infer emotion, agreement, or personality from a keyword, a sentence form, or a single response that simply keeps the conversation going. A changed judgment needs evidence in the interaction, not merely a seemingly applicable theory.

## Public guide and local configuration

This file is for users and host models. The daily-review template links here. The instance's `biblia` record remains user-maintained; configuration neither imports this guide nor overwrites existing reading notes.

Locally, map “question → chosen literature → file or lawful access link → sections to read.” Keep actual reading notes, private examples, and analyses within your instance. The guide is not a complete procedure and does not imply that CA can automatically determine a unique meaning for every sentence.
